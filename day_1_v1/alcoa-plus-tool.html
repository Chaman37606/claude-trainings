What is ALCOA+?

ALCOA+ is an international data integrity standard that ensures all records in pharmaceutical operations maintain the highest level of quality, accuracy, and compliance. It builds upon the original ALCOA principles with additional requirements for modern operational environments.

Key Purpose: ALCOA+ ensures that pharmaceutical and operational data is reliable, traceable, and verifiable throughout its lifecycle.

Core Benefits

  • Regulatory Compliance: Meets FDA, EMA, and ICH requirements
  • Product Quality: Ensures consistency and safety of pharmaceutical products
  • Risk Management: Identifies and mitigates data integrity risks
  • Audit Trail: Maintains complete electronic records with full traceability
  • Operational Excellence: Establishes standardized data practices across teams

Who Should Use This?

  • Manufacturing and Quality Assurance teams
  • Laboratory and Testing personnel
  • Process operations and maintenance staff
  • Data management and IT support
  • Regulatory and compliance departments
Principle Definition Key Requirement
Attributable Who performed the action? User identification, timestamps, electronic signatures
Legible Is the data clear and readable? Permanent, indelible records; proper formatting
Contemporaneous When was it recorded? Real-time entry; no backfilling or retroactive changes
Original Is this the first/primary record? First entry preserved; copies properly marked
Accurate Is the data correct? Data verification, SOPs, training, error correction
Complete Are all required fields captured? No missing data; all supporting documentation included
Consistent Is data uniform across systems? Same data referenced consistently; reconciliation performed
Enduring Will records persist long-term? Secure storage, backup strategies, retention compliance
Available Can authorized users access records? Accessible retrieval; proper access controls; disaster recovery

Attributable

WHO performed the action?

Every action and record must be linked to the person who created or modified it, with an unambiguous identification.

  • User IDs and electronic signatures
  • Timestamp with timezone information
  • Role-based access control (RBAC)
  • Audit logs for all changes

Test Data Example

User: JSmith001 | Timestamp: 2026-08-29 14:32:45 UTC | Action: Created batch record

Audit Trail: Record ID: BR-2026-08-001, Created by: John Smith, Date: 08/29/2026, Time: 2:32 PM EDT

Electronic Signature: /es/ JSmith001 08/29/2026 14:32:45

Legible

Is the data clear and readable?

Data must be recorded in a permanent, indelible format that remains clear and understandable throughout its retention period.

  • Permanent ink or electronic equivalents
  • Clear formatting and standardization
  • No erasures or use of correction fluid
  • Proper character encoding for digital records

Test Data Example

Original Record: Temperature Reading: 72.3°C | Pressure: 2.5 bar

Amendment Log: Original value → Amended value "72.5°C" by MJones | 08/29/2026 15:45

Format Check: UTF-8 encoded, indexed fully searchable, never overwritten or deleted

Contemporaneous

WHEN was it recorded?

Records must be created at the time the event occurs, not retroactively. Real-time data entry ensures accuracy and prevents manipulation.

  • Real-time data entry at point of action
  • No backfilling of historical data
  • System-generated timestamps
  • Limited amendment windows

Test Data Example

Event: Batch MFG-2026-08-001 started | Recorded: 08/29/2026 08:00:15 (within 1 minute of event)

Rejection Test: Attempt to backdate entry to 08/28/2026 → System blocks and logs attempt

Amendment Window: 30-day window for corrections with full justification required

Original

Is this the first/primary record?

The original record must be preserved and protected. Copies or data transfers must be properly identified and verified.

  • Primary source documents retained
  • Digital signatures on copies
  • Chain of custody documentation
  • Version control and data lineage

Test Data Example

Original: Batch Record BR-001 created 08/20/2026, marked as ORIGINAL

Copy: BR-001_COPY_Archive created 08/25/2026, digitally signed by DSmith001

Chain of Custody: Original → Regulatory Review → Compliance Verified → Archive (all logged)

Accurate

Is the data correct?

Data must be verified for accuracy through established procedures, training, and error detection mechanisms.

  • Documented SOPs for data entry
  • Verification and validation protocols
  • Staff training and competency
  • Error investigation and CAPA

Complete

Are all required fields captured?

All mandatory information must be recorded with no missing data. Supporting documentation must be included and linked.

  • Mandatory field requirements in systems
  • All supporting attachments included
  • Cross-reference documentation
  • Reconciliation of all data elements

Consistent

Is data uniform across systems?

The same data must be consistent across all systems and locations where it appears, with reconciliation and verification.

  • Data integration and reconciliation
  • Standardized data formats
  • Cross-system validation
  • Master data management

Enduring

Will records persist long-term?

Records must be protected, backed up, and retained for the required period with integrity assured throughout.

  • Secure data storage and encryption
  • Regular backup procedures
  • Disaster recovery plans
  • Retention policy compliance

Available

Can authorized users access records?

Authorized personnel must be able to retrieve and review records efficiently while maintaining security and access controls.

  • User access controls and permissions
  • Efficient search and retrieval systems
  • Audit trail review capabilities
  • System performance and uptime

Common Audit Findings & Preventive Measures

❌ Finding: Missing or Incomplete Audit Trails

Prevention: Implement system-enforced audit logging, regular audits of audit trail functionality, and staff training on the importance of complete records.

❌ Finding: Retroactive Data Entry or Modification

Prevention: Enforce real-time data entry through system design, disable backdating capabilities, and require written justification for any amendments.

❌ Finding: Inadequate User Access Controls

Prevention: Implement role-based access control, regular access reviews, segregation of duties, and periodic competency verification.

❌ Finding: Insufficient Training Documentation

Prevention: Develop comprehensive training programs, maintain records of training completion, conduct competency assessments, and provide refresher training.

❌ Finding: No System Validation or Change Control

Prevention: Establish rigorous validation and change control procedures, document all system changes, and verify system integrity after updates.

❌ Finding: Inadequate Data Backup and Recovery

Prevention: Implement automated backup procedures, test recovery regularly, maintain offsite backups, and document disaster recovery plans.

Success Tip: Establish a Data Integrity Council that meets regularly to review compliance metrics, address findings, and drive continuous improvement across the organization.
